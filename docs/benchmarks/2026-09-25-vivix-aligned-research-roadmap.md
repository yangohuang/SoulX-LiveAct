# 4090 long-horizon streaming-video research roadmap

## Thesis

Use SoulX-LiveAct as a controlled, public testbed for the research question: **how should a causal audio-driven video generator trade temporal continuity, expressive movement, identity/lip fidelity and inference cost over long streams?** A visible jump is one diagnostic case, not the project's success criterion. A research result may be a failed method with a convincing causal explanation and reproducible evidence. It does not require an upstream SoulX PR.

The ordering follows the Vivix JD's emphasis on long-form stability, error accumulation, efficient diffusion sampling and independent experimentation. [Vivix-W1](https://vivix.ai/tech-report-vivix-w1) names streaming generation, low-step acceleration, long-horizon consistency and multimodal reference stability as targets; its proprietary implementation is not available here.

| Milestone | Feasible 4090 work | Exit evidence | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Multi-objective measurement | Finish three identities × two seeds × 30 seconds for baseline and one intervention. Track chunk-window peaks and offsets, motion amount, worst-window visual reviews, SyncNet, identity drift and chunk time separately. | Frozen input manifest, video/JSON/timeline artifacts, per-identity table, detector coverage and failure cases. | One metric cannot stand in for natural motion or audiovisual quality. |
| 2. Error-source ablation | Change one inference factor at a time: history representation/precision, memory availability, and two- versus three-step sampling. Preserve audio, prompt, seed and output resolution within each pair. | Which changes affect first predicted clean latent, decoded seam, or only latency; compute-quality frontier. | A correlation at a boundary is not proof of the model's training-time failure mechanism. |
| 3. One algorithmic candidate | Select from the replicated failure mode: if errors are sparse at boundaries, test a causal overlap/resampling fallback with its extra DiT cost measured; if reference drift grows with time, test reference/history refresh under a fixed cache budget. Compare with baseline and fixed anchor on held-out identities. The fixed anchor itself is not a default candidate after visible hand ghosting. | Lower severe-event rate *and* no new delayed peak, motion suppression, identity or SyncNet loss on held-out identities; report extra model passes and latency. If not met, report rejection. | No claim of SRF, physics-aware motion or real-time 24 FPS. |
| 4. Training hypothesis only after a valid target exists | Use a smaller reference/audio-conditioned generator or distributional teacher to test generated-history training. Measure one-card memory before training. | Same-seed, held-out-identity long-rollout improvement with visual and lip-sync review. | Text-only Wan flow MSE is insufficient to validate LiveAct forcing. |

The [three-identity, one-seed pilot](2026-09-25-long-horizon-stability-study.md) and [second-seed replication](2026-09-25-seed43-replication.md) complete the six-pair *measurement* gate of Milestone 1. Fixed anchoring lowers boundary-window grayscale peaks in all six cases, but also reduces local image change, worsens all six SyncNet relative-confidence scores and visibly smears identity-2 hands at both seeds. It is therefore a rejected quality candidate, not a default method for Milestone 3. The [velocity-aware latent extrapolation ablation](2026-09-25-velocity-aware-anchor-ablation.md) also failed the predeclared hand/lip gate on the development identity, so it was not run on a held-out identity. The [pre-output latent-signal study](2026-09-25-pre-output-boundary-signal-study.md) measured 22 boundaries in two unanchored seeds and a known visual-failure control: final-step gap MAE tracked immediate pixel change but ranked the visibly smeared hand case *below* the clean raised-hand case. It is retained as a diagnostic, rejected as a selective-fallback trigger; no fallback was implemented. The worst-window visual audit and pose-sensitive identity proxy still have limits, and 30 seconds cannot establish an infinite-video error slope. The next useful step is source attribution at a known bad window—separate denoising correction, generated-history state and VAE/context decoding—before designing a new detector or spending more GPU on fallbacks. Do not continue tuning unconditional latent blends.

The [block-16 source-attribution replay](2026-09-25-hand-artifact-source-attribution.md) completes a first targeted version of that next step. Same-arm VAE replay matches the source MP4 within 2.7/255 mean RGB error; holding velocity history fixed, removing only the final-step correction eliminates the conspicuous hand smear in the eight-frame offline replay. Cross-history swaps indicate that the post-anchor current block, not VAE history alone, carries this artifact. This is a mechanism localization for one case, not a tested full-rollout correction. A further causal study would selectively disable the final correction in a full 30-second run and evaluate motion, SyncNet and identity on held-out identities before reconsidering any intervention. The prior six-pair rejection still stands.

That [full-rollout skip-final ablation](2026-09-25-final-step-anchor-ablation.md) is now complete on the identity-2 development case. The window-500 white/dark hand trail is absent and SyncNet relative confidence recovers from 6.224 to 6.950 (baseline 6.955), but the mean eight-transition motion sum remains only 84.3% of unanchored baseline, below the predeclared 90% gate. The candidate was rejected before a held-out identity run. This is useful source attribution and step-schedule evidence, not a stable long-video solution. Further work should move toward a conditional motion/reference signal with perceptual supervision or an aligned training target, rather than tune more unconditional latent anchors on the same case.

The [90-second causal baseline and 60-second second-identity check](2026-09-25-90s-causal-stability-baseline.md) extend the measurement horizon without intervention. Neither trajectory triggers the preregistered late movement/face-proxy screen; the block-time-only throughput remains about 1.6–1.7 generated FPS on a 4090. SyncNet relative confidence declines across 30-second bins on both examples, strongly in the 90-second talking case (7.122→6.413) and weakly in the 60-second singing/guitar case (5.779→5.619), but audio content is not matched across bins. This is an open hypothesis, not proven error accumulation. Next, repeat the same speech content within a long causal rollout and compare late versus early SyncNet and mouth motion, ideally also against a fresh-history control. This controlled content test is more informative than adding another unconditional latent anchor.

That [repeated-speech 90-second experiment](2026-09-25-repeat-speech-history-test.md) is complete. Three bitwise-identical 30-second PCM periods produced SyncNet confidences 6.992/6.842/6.792, with an independent same-audio fresh-history score of 6.971. The 0.199 late–early decrease falls below the predeclared 0.5 signal threshold; excluding two seconds at each period edge also fails (6.953→6.695). Lower-face motion is stable. Critically, the full-WAV SoX/Wav2Vec pipeline maps the repeated raw periods to different model conditions: interior mean feature cosine is only 0.943/0.950 relative to the first period, below the predeclared 0.99 matching rule. No history-accumulation inference or second-seed run was justified. The next experiment should move to a failure mode with stronger replicated evidence, then control the *actual audio embeddings* if testing history causality.

The first two milestones can yield a credible Vivix interview story without improving the model: an explicit long-video failure taxonomy, controlled ablations, a speed/quality budget, and honest negative findings. Milestone 3 would be the stronger algorithmic contribution if it passes. The personal fork can host code and artifacts throughout; an official PR is a separate decision after user review and after an actual reusable contribution passes its quality gate.

The existing [4090 resolution sweep](2026-09-24-liveact-4090-resolution-sweep.md) already supplies a compute anchor for milestone 2: 416×720 at three steps generated about 1.69 FPS, while 224×384 at two steps reached about 6.49 FPS, still short of 24 FPS and without a matched quality assessment. The new stability evaluator should be reused on any reduced-step or reduced-resolution candidate before calling it a usable speedup.

The [matched 416×720 two-step frontier](2026-09-25-two-step-quality-frontier.md) now closes the simplest algorithmic-sampling question. Dropping the middle denoising step improves later-block time 21.388→14.895 seconds (1.436×), but matched-window SyncNet confidence falls 6.930→6.318, exceeding the predeclared 0.30 loss gate. Boundary motion and face coverage pass, which makes the lip failure a meaningful separate quality axis. The candidate was rejected before identity holdouts, and block-only throughput is only 2.095 FPS. For a future efficiency study, first preserve the two audio-conditioned passes and profile a targeted transfer/compute change; otherwise use a separately validated low-step training or distillation method. Do not expand unsupported two-step tuning on the same identity merely to claim a favorable speed number.

The [pinned-weight transfer A/B](2026-09-25-pinned-block-weight-ab.md) tested that quality-preserving-in-principle runtime route without changing the three-step schedule. Pinning existing FP8 DiT block weights improves 224×384 later-block time by 23.4%, but improves the target 416×720 only 13.0% while reducing minimum sampled host availability from 36.1 to 11.2 GiB. Fresh profiler traces show that weight-copy CPU time collapses, while FP8 GEMM time is similar; the end-to-end target-resolution return on host memory is still weak. The target-resolution preregistered speed gate fails, so this is an optional configuration trade-off rather than a new default or official PR proposal. Exact decoded output identity was not established; same-configuration reruns also differ numerically.

The [fixed-seed reproducibility diagnostic](2026-09-25-fixed-seed-determinism-diagnostic.md) explains that same-configuration variation: across independent default-process runs, Wav2Vec features and reference VAE latent diverge **before** DiT despite identical source media, CLIP condition and seeded noise. A factorial cuDNN test found that disabling `cudnn.benchmark` alone yielded identical 35-boundary fingerprints and MP4 hashes across independent runs; setting `cudnn.deterministic=True` while leaving benchmark on did not. An opt-in flag now controls the policy without changing the default; the 416×720 five-second steady-block difference was only +0.25–0.43% in two flagged runs versus one fresh default. Future A/B quality studies should use the flag in every arm.

The [deterministic two-seed sampling recheck](2026-09-25-deterministic-step-quality-recheck.md) repeats the 416×720, 30-second comparison with that control. Seeds 43 and 44 both show about 30.3% lower later-block time from three to two steps, but SyncNet confidence drops 0.667 and 0.430 on identical 747-window support, failing the ≤0.30 quality gate twice; motion and face proxies pass. Thus the existing two-step schedule is rejected with replicated evidence on this identity/audio. The code reveals that the change also removes one of two audio-conditioned DiT calls. A sharply scoped next candidate is a two-step schedule with audio conditioning on both calls: first verify a real implementation and latency, then test lip, motion and worst windows under the same deterministic control, and only pursue an identity holdout if the development gate passes. This is an inference-schedule hypothesis, not an established Vivix-W1 method or a real-time solution.

That [first-audio two-step ablation](2026-09-25-two-step-audio-conditioning.md) now tests the candidate at fixed two-step timesteps. Seed 43 recovers SyncNet from stock-two 6.271 to 6.868 (three-step 6.938), keeps 100.9% of three-step boundary motion, and is 28.7% faster by later-block time. The preregistered seed-44 replication also recovers SyncNet (6.214→6.782, three-step 6.644) and is 29.1% faster, but retains only **78.2%** of three-step boundary motion versus the 90% floor; sparse images also show smaller gestures. Hence the simple first-audio policy is rejected as a full-quality default before identity holdout. The replicated lip response is still a useful mechanism signal: sound conditioning at the high-noise call changes more than timing while adding only about 2% block time over stock two-step. A future candidate should explicitly balance speech fidelity and body expressiveness instead of optimizing SyncNet alone, and must be predeclared on a fresh evaluation case. Do not present either two-step variant as an upstream PR-ready solution or real time on one 4090.

The [spatial motion-source audit](2026-09-25-spatial-motion-source-audit.md) then separates that failed full-frame boundary proxy into mouth, head, lower-body and background masks, plus optical flow and visibility-filtered wrist trajectories. On seed 44 the new policy's lower-body flow is 0.886× three-step near boundaries but 1.136× outside them; head and background also contribute to the whole-frame image-change drop. Tracked wrist displacement has no consistent left/right reduction. Thus keep the original gate failure while avoid saying all-time body expression is proven suppressed. Before another quality claim, predeclare a better body/gesture metric on fresh identities and examine full videos; do not simply relax the existing 90% threshold after seeing this diagnostic.
