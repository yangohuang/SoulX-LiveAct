# Exact audio conditioning in a 90-second causal LiveAct rollout

The earlier [repeated-PCM experiment](2026-09-25-repeat-speech-history-test.md) did **not** control the model's Wav2Vec input: after full-WAV SoX tempo and encoding, corresponding 30-second periods had mean feature cosine only 0.943/0.950. This [separate, preregistered experiment](../superpowers/specs/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning-horizon.md) repeats one canonical **model feature period** rather than assuming repeated PCM is enough. It asks whether a late lip-sync trend remains when the actual DiT audio condition is matched in period interiors. The previous experiment's weak late–early decrease remains a negative result; this is not a post hoc reclassification.

## Controlled input and implementation

Image 2 (SHA-256 `7f9c1d2567ca391200b1a76b2e07442111d4472493411b59195f80872d078550`) and one exact 30-second mono PCM16 source (SHA-256 `85372c99f11ca8396d9c01bb1aa1314bb165e93000938ba87f8ad06de104637e`) are held fixed. The 90-second request audio is the same 480,000 raw samples concatenated three times, SHA-256 `2df37c1e0e5c4ee22c1cd6cc2839fb23b08954f5bc1a58a86e6c70216b2454b0`. The output MP4 uses this request audio; the new default-off `--audio_embedding_repeat_source` flag separately encodes the 30-second source through LiveAct's SoX tempo/Wav2Vec path and tiles its BF16 feature tensor to the request's normal feature length. The 30-second fresh-history control uses the same source path. Both arms use seed 43, prompt `一个人在说话`, 416×720 at 24 FPS, three denoising steps, FP8 GEMM/KV, CPU block/cache offload, `resident_kv_steps=1`, offline FP8/T5 caches, no compile/pinning/latent anchor and `--disable_cudnn_benchmark`. The historical 90-second video did not use this feature intervention or the deterministic cuDNN flag, so it is context, not a matched experimental arm.

The feature sidecar records raw SHA-256 for each 720-frame period and a separate hash of the feature span feeding each period's interior ±2-frame audio windows. This excludes two seconds at each edge, where `get_audio_emb`'s global clamping/context cannot be made identical by naive tiling. Identical hashes prove the *feature values* match on this machine; they do not hold model noise, pose, hidden state or generated history constant.

## Long rollout integrity and input equality

The [continuous 90-second video](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90.mp4) has SHA-256 `51391a6cdda131ac120ade069504d57f441d170fa97be69174d1b18da2c4366b`. FFprobe reports 2162 frames at 24 FPS, a 90.083-second video stream and a 90.000-second/16-kHz audio stream. The [resource record](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90-resources.json) has 68 completed blocks, 19.161-second later-block median, 16,004-MiB maximum sampled GPU process memory and 21,791-MiB minimum machine-wide MemAvailable. Block time alone corresponds to about 1.67 generated FPS; the startup and final mux make end-to-end throughput lower. No real-time claim follows.

The [feature sidecar](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90.audio-embedding.json) records a canonical 720×12×768 BF16 tensor tiled to 2160×12×768. All three period hashes are `6f6efa014b8cde63ebf475a7e84cb4c47d87f198b1dd117bbd653d6c6c7c8be0`; all three interior-plus-context hashes are `f31b20165c7eddfea2801fc74ac4a4964b2509c179cb19e06ef2ac088ff72be3`. The [request](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90-request.json) and [compressed log](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90.log.gz) preserve invocation and block timings.

The [independent fresh-history 30-second video](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-fresh30.mp4) has SHA-256 `5124efdbcb70ac10f2ffdeaa50e7cac4a8d7f010c41c4b6da38b0102f1b34645`, 722 frames at 24 FPS, 30.083-second video and 30.000-second audio. Its [feature sidecar](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-fresh30.audio-embedding.json) has the **same canonical period and interior-context SHA-256** as each long-run period. Its [resource record](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-fresh30-resources.json) has 23 completed blocks, 19.182-second later-block median and 16,004-MiB sampled GPU maximum. Both MP4s fully decoded with FFmpeg; the sparse [90-second](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90-contact.png) and [fresh-30-second](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-fresh30-contact.png) contacts show the same character without a gross identity or background change.

The [first-30-second frame comparison](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-first30-frame-comparison.json) adds a useful limit to the hash claim: long versus fresh outputs are pixel-identical for the first **662/722** frames, then differ for the final 60 frames (mean RGB MAE 3.668/255 there). This begins at a generation-block boundary near the request endpoint. The feature sidecars prove equality of the underlying 30-second BF16 tensor and per-frame interior ±2 context, **not** equality of every 53-frame block input at the fresh request's endpoint, its hidden state or final pixels. We did not isolate the last-block difference's cause. The prespecified fresh-control SyncNet score is still close to the long early score, but the fresh MP4 must not be described as bitwise identical to the full first 30 seconds.

A later [instrumented request-end audit](2026-09-25-terminal-horizon-divergence.md) found two compatible mechanisms in shorter matched pairs: the terminal `get_audio_emb` block clamps different positions in a short request, changing its final latent, while H.264 can make encoded pixels differ before the first changed pre-encoder frame. The original 30/90 clips lack pre-encoder hashes, so this is mechanistic supporting evidence, not direct attribution of their exact first divergent raw frame.

## Prespecified quality screen

The same SyncNet v2/S3FD crop policy produced [long-run](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90-syncnet.json) and [fresh-control](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-fresh30-syncnet.json) score JSON, with archived [long](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90-syncnet-distances.npz) and [fresh](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-fresh30-syncnet-distances.npz) 31-shift matrices and corresponding face-crop AVIs. The long rollout's late–early decreases are **0.189 full and 0.259 interior**, both below the preregistered **0.5** signal threshold. All three bins exceed the 90%-of-750 support minimum, with the same −2 offset. The fresh control differs from the long early bin by 0.003 full and 0.023 interior, within the 0.3 comparability limit. The [raw boundary record](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90-motion.json), [segment trend](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90-trend.json) and [chart](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90-trend.png) give a late/early median peak ratio of **1.143**, below 1.25, and motion-sum ratio of **1.037**, above the 0.90 floor. [InsightFace CPU sampling](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-face.json) found exactly one face in 91/91 long and 31/31 fresh one-second samples; long early/late reference-face mean cosine is 0.6967/0.6877, decline 0.0090, below 0.05.

The [long](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90-mouth.json) and [fresh](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-fresh30-mouth.json) fixed-crop mouth-motion records show no coarse collapse (long 6.284→6.267, fresh 6.279). The [three largest boundary windows](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-seed43-repeat90-worst-windows.png) at frames 500, 788 and 1556 show fast pose/hand movement without an obvious new gross ghost in the eight-frame strips. Matched speech-position [5-s](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-mouth-5s.png), [15-s](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-mouth-15s.png) and [25-s](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/liveact-exact-embedding-mouth-25s.png) mouth strips retain visible opening/closing in each period. These sparse visual and fixed-ROI checks cannot establish perceptual equivalence or phoneme-level accuracy.

**Decision: negative screen; stop before seed 44.** Exact repeated model audio removed the previous input mismatch, but this identity/seed/audio did not exhibit the preregistered late lip-sync loss, and the other monitored proxies did not reveal a late failure. This narrows the earlier different-speech 90-second decline from an apparent universal history problem to a content/run-dependent observation; it does **not** prove history can never hurt, nor solve LiveAct continuity. The feature-tiling switch is an experimental control for this fork, not a quality improvement to submit upstream. A stronger failure case would justify a matched-conditional, multi-seed study before training-time forcing is proposed.

The [artifact manifest](artifacts/2026-09-25-exact-audio-conditioning/manifest.json) lists sizes and SHA-256 for the inputs and 40 archived artifacts. The MP4s, SyncNet face crops, distance matrices, JSON, plots and compressed execution logs are retained for independent inspection.

## Prospective interpretation

The written screen requires the 90-second rollout's late–early SyncNet relative-confidence decline to be at least 0.5 in both full 30-second bins and bins trimmed two seconds at each edge, with stable best offset and an independent fresh-history score within 0.3 of the early bin. Every bin needs at least 90% of the nominal 750 scored windows. Boundary-peak late/early ratio must remain ≤1.25, local motion retention ≥90%, single-face coverage ≥90%, reference-face late decline ≤0.05, and mouth/video review must not reveal obvious pose/crop confounding. A screen pass would be a *candidate* time/history signal requiring another seed, not proof of forcing or exposure bias. A failure leaves the long-history hypothesis unproven and stops the next seed.
