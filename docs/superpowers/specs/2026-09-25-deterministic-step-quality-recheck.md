# Deterministic-condition two-step quality recheck

The earlier 416×720/30-second same-seed two-versus-three-step A/B observed a 1.436× later-block speedup and a SyncNet confidence decline of 0.612, exceeding its 0.30 quality gate. A later audit found that default cuDNN benchmarking makes independent same-seed processes receive different Wav2Vec and VAE conditions before DiT. This recheck asks whether the **observed step-quality trade-off persists after those conditions are controlled**, not whether two steps are universally bad.

Freeze image 2, prompt `一个人在说话`, seed 43, the same exact 30-second PCM WAV (SHA-256 `85372c99f11ca8396d9c01bb1aa1314bb165e93000938ba87f8ad06de104637e`), 416×720/24 FPS, FP8 GEMM/KV, CPU cache and block offload, `resident_kv_steps=0`, offline FP8/T5 caches, no compile/pin/anchor, and add `--disable_cudnn_benchmark` to **both** arms. Only the schedule changes: three steps `[1000, 937.5, 833.33333333, 0]` versus two `[1000, 833.33333333, 0]`. Generate a fresh 30-second video for each arm in the same checkout. Verify 722 decoded frames and 30-second audio, log all 23 block costs, and check sample GPU/host memory.

Reapply the original prospective gate: later-block median at least 20% faster, mean eight-transition boundary motion sum at least 90% of the three-step control, mean boundary peak no more than 125%, SyncNet confidence decline no more than **0.30** on comparable audio-supported face crops with stable best offset, single-face detection at least 90%, reference-face cosine decline no more than 0.03, and no new conspicuous defect in seven timepoints and five worst boundary windows. Use the same motion, face and SyncNet pipelines as before. A one-seed gate failure is an observed quality rejection. If the deterministic seed43 SyncNet drop again exceeds 0.30 while other control checks pass, run **seed44 on the same identity** with both schedules to test replication; only a second failure supports a repeated step-quality effect. If seed43 passes, do not run seed44 until a new hypothesis is written, and revise the earlier causal wording.

The flag controls cuDNN algorithm selection only on the tested software/hardware. It does not guarantee equivalence across versions or make separate denoising schedules pixel-aligned.
