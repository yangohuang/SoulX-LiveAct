# Two-step first-audio conditioning ablation

The deterministic [two-seed schedule recheck](../../benchmarks/2026-09-25-deterministic-step-quality-recheck.md) found a repeatable 30.3% later-block speed gain from the existing three-to-two-step change, but SyncNet confidence fell by 0.667 and 0.430 on the same image/audio. The schedules differ in both the integration path and the count of audio-conditioned DiT calls: three steps use `skip_audio=(True, False, False)`, two use `(True, False)`. This study asks whether **adding audio conditioning to the first of the existing two calls** can restore lip synchronization without losing the speed gain. It does not assert that the original failure was solely caused by audio-call count.

## Design choice

Keep the existing two-step timesteps `(1000, 833.33333333, 0)` and every other inference setting fixed. Add an opt-in `--audio_first_step` flag. With exactly two denoising steps, the new schedule uses `skip_audio=(False, False)`; no flag preserves `(True, False)` byte for byte. Reject the flag with three steps to keep the experimental meaning unambiguous. Pass it through the monitored benchmark harness. Do not alter model weights, audio features, cached prompts, default schedule, or already recorded baselines.

Alternatives considered: moving the middle timestep while retaining one conditioned call would confound integration and audio-call count; an audio-activity-based per-block router would introduce switching artifacts and a threshold requiring separate validation. The fixed two-call ablation changes one factor and is the smallest useful first test.

## Preregistered experiment

First run a deterministic 5-second smoke test at 416×720/24 FPS, image 2, seed 43 and the existing FP8 GEMM/KV plus CPU offload setup. Check complete decode, no runtime error or obvious gross face/hand defect, and sampled GPU memory below the 24-GiB card. If this fails, stop and document it. Otherwise run a new 30-second image-2/seed-43 video with the exact PCM, prompt, resolution and offload policy of the archived deterministic frontier. Reuse its seed-43 three-step and default-two-step videos as matched controls; verify the exact image/audio hashes and flag-controlled cuDNN policy from their request/resource files. The one new arm must use `--disable_cudnn_benchmark`, no pinning/compile/anchor, and `resident_kv_steps=0`.

Accept the candidate on the development seed only if all of these hold: later-block median at least 20% lower than the archived three-step control; SyncNet confidence on the same 747-window support no more than **0.30 below** three steps, with best offset unchanged and at least 0.35 recovery relative to the existing two-step arm; mean eight-transition boundary motion sum at least 90% of the three-step control, mean peak no more than 125%; single-face detection at least 90%, mean reference-face cosine no more than 0.03 below baseline; and no new conspicuous defect in seven timepoints or five worst boundary windows. Record full video, per-block timing, sampled memory, SyncNet matrix, motion/face JSON and sheets.

If the development seed passes all gates, run seed 44 under the same arm and compare to the already archived seed-44 controls. Require the same gate on seed 44 before considering an identity holdout in a new written experiment. If seed 43 fails any gate, stop and report a rejected mechanism test; do not tune timesteps or quality thresholds on this identity after seeing the result. An improved proxy score does not establish actual lip fidelity without video review. Even a pass would remain far from real-time 24 FPS on one 4090 and would not be an upstream quality PR without broader evidence and user review.
